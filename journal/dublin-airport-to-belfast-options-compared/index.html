Dublin Airport to Belfast: every option compared

About 105 miles, two hours by road, no border stop. The question is how — and it depends on how many of you there are, how much luggage you have and what time you land.

Coach: cheap, but not door to door

Aircoach and Translink run direct services from the airport to Belfast. It's the cheapest option for one person travelling light in daytime. Downsides: you wait for the next departure, it stops in central Belfast (Europa/Glengall Street) rather than at your hotel, and late-night frequency drops. With two or more people and bags, the coach plus an onward taxi starts to approach the cost of a private transfer.

Train: the scenic wrong answer

The Enterprise from Dublin Connolly to Belfast Lanyon Place is a good train — but the airport isn't on it. You'd take a bus or taxi to Connolly first, then another taxi at the Belfast end. Three legs with luggage.

Taxi: unpredictable

Dublin airport taxis can take you to Belfast, but many drivers decline a cross-border fare, the meter runs regardless of traffic, and nobody is tracking your flight. Expect €250–350.

Private chauffeur: fixed, tracked, met in arrivals

A pre-booked chauffeur meets you in the arrivals hall of your terminal, has tracked your flight, loads the luggage and drives you to your door in Belfast — or Bangor, Newcastle, Derry or the Causeway Coast. The price is agreed before you fly and doesn't change if the M1 is slow. From £220 per vehicle for up to two, from £260 for up to six confirm. Book a Dublin Airport to Belfast transfer.

Practical notes

  • There is no border check on the road; you may not notice crossing.
  • Currency changes: euro in the Republic, sterling in Northern Ireland. Cards work everywhere.
  • Rush hour around Dublin (07:00–09:30, 16:00–19:00) adds 20–30 minutes.
  • Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 are a short walk apart; tell your driver which.
